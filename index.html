fiscal

Typed e-invoice models generated from the official XML schemas, written in Spiral.

Governments that mandate electronic invoicing publish their invoice formats as XSD schemas. fiscal reads those schemas and generates, for every document family, a Spiral module with:

  • the document as types: records for sequences, unions for choices and enumerations;
  • a parser and a printer for each record (*_of_xml, *_to_xml);
  • a validator for every facet-constrained value (*_is_valid), with patterns compiled to DFA tables;
  • an example document builder (*_example), whose output is checked against the official XSD.

The XML reader, the XSD model, include/import resolution and the regex-to-DFA compiler live in Spiral's own library (lib/spiral: xml, xsd, xsd_resolve, xsd_regex). This repository holds the generator, the corpus checks and the schema families it targets.

Reports

Every CI run on main rebuilds these from the official sources and publishes them with this page:

  • `target/ci-metrics/reports.md`: the run at a glance, with its commit, run and build time, the test counts and every test that did not pass, the slowest files, the bench and every example's verdict. src/reports.spi renders it from the tables below as typed rows.
  • `target/ci-metrics/bench.tsv`: parse and write medians (µs) of each generated unit reading back and re-writing its own example, 31 runs per unit.
  • `target/ci-metrics/examples.tsv`: every unit's example checked against the official XSD; SignaturePending counts the ds:Signature an unsigned document is still waiting for.
  • `target/ci-metrics/tests.tsv` and `files.tsv`: every test row per backend target, and the time each module took.
  • `samples/`: the example documents the generated code writes.
  • `canon/en16931-terms.json`: the EN 16931 business terms and groups with their UBL paths, taken from the OpenPeppol BIS Billing 3.0.20 syntax bindings; each term has its own cardinality and its effective one, counting its optional or repeated ancestors.
  • `canon/pint-terms.json`: the Peppol PINT Billing 1.1.3 terms (IBT/IBG) with their invoice and credit-note bindings, and the PINT terms that have no EN 16931 row.

The four clearance platforms together handle roughly 24 billion documents a year (derived; their scopes differ: CFDI also covers payroll and payment receipts, and KSeF is not universal until 2027).

Two limits on the EU row:

  • It is reach for sending to the public sector. Every EU public buyer must accept EN 16931 in both of its syntaxes, so UBL output reaches all of them. National B2B mandates add their own profiles (XRechnung, CIUS-RO, HR-CIUS, CIUS-PT, Peppol BIS), and some accept only CII or Factur-X, which fiscal does not generate.
  • B2B arrives with ViDA. Under VAT in the Digital Age (Directive (EU) 2025/516), member states may mandate domestic e-invoicing without a derogation since 14 April 2025, EN 16931 e-invoices with digital reporting become the default for intra-EU B2B on 1 July 2030, and national systems must align by 1 January 2035. The Commission estimates it cuts VAT fraud by up to EUR 11 bn a year, against an EU VAT gap of EUR 128 bn (9.5%) in 2023.

Potential

Outside what is covered, 26 jurisdictions with live or announced mandates add 33.98% of world GDP and 48.79% of world population (derived from World Bank 2025). With today's reach that is 55.4% of world GDP and 58.5% of the world's population under some form of mandated e-invoicing or continuous transaction control. The United States (26.0% of world GDP) has no federal mandate.

Market context

  • B2B e-invoices worldwide: about 87 bn of roughly 300 bn B2B invoices in 2026 (29%), forecast at 107 bn by 2030 (Billentis, a vendor-sponsored report).
  • Commercial estimates put the e-invoicing software market at roughly US$18-25 bn in 2025 (IMARC, TBRC).
  • Brazil's consumption-tax reform (LC 214/2025) adds IBS/CBS groups to every Brazilian document from 2026 (test rates) through 2033, when ICMS and ISS end, so the NF-e family keeps gaining layout versions; regenerating from the published schema is how this project follows them.

How it works

deps/corpus/*.xsd ──xml.read_xml──▶ xsd.read_schema ──xsd_resolve──▶ schema tree ──generator.spi──▶ src/generated_*.spi
                                                                                                        │
                                          samples/generated_*_example.xml ◀── each module's example test ┘
  • src/generator.spi maps a resolved schema to Spiral: sequences become records (occurs become option or list), choices and enumerations become unions (shared by value set), facet-constrained strings get validators whose patterns are compiled to DFA tables by xsd_regex. Its tests write every generated module to generated/<module>/, each beside a one-module spiral.toml, so a test cell type-checks only its own module.
  • Each generated module carries its own test, which builds the module's example document and writes it to samples/ (the NF-e 4.00 module also parses an address back and checks the XML round trip). scripts/family-gates.ps1 then validates the examples against the official XSDs with .NET's XmlSchemaSet. SAF-T PT is left to its typed reader, because its schema is XSD 1.1 (assertions), which XmlSchemaSet does not read; five examples with open defects (three Costa Rica Canton values, the Chilean EnvioDTE and VeriFactu namespaces) join once fixed.
  • src/corpus_checks.spi pins the reader and the regex engine to independent oracles over the whole corpus: element and attribute counts against .NET's XML DOM, include resolution against XmlSchemaSet (scripts/resolve-check.ps1), and every distinct pattern of every corpus schema against .NET regex on generated trial strings: 336, of which 196 are Brazilian and 140 come from the other families (scripts/pattern-oracle.ps1 derives the list into src/patterns.txt).

Building

pwsh scripts/init.ps1      # deps/spiral (a sibling spiral checkout, or a clone) and the schema corpus in deps/corpus
pwsh scripts/build.ps1     # pattern oracle, generator (writes generated/), then every test file through Cells
pwsh scripts/publish.ps1   # dist/: the source tree and the example documents as a browsable page

build.ps1 -Files generator,corpus_checks runs a subset. CI (.github/workflows/gh-pages.yml) runs the same three steps on Ubuntu and Windows and publishes dist to GitHub Pages.

Generated modules are committed, formatted: build.ps1 regenerates them before it tests and fails when they differ from the committed copy. Example documents and the pattern oracle's output are build products, ignored by git.

Known limits

  • EU coverage is UBL only. CII and its Factur-X / ZUGFeRD profiles (France's 2026 B2B mandate, Germany) are the next system: the CII D16B schemas and examples are pinned (deps/corpus/eu/en16931-cii) and the six-profile lattice is modeled as types (src/profiles.spi), but no CII family is generated yet.
  • Facet-constrained values are plain string aliases, and the generated readers do not call their validators, so an invalid value reads without error. Each constrained type is planned to become a nominal type built only through its validator, which makes a raw string where a validated value is expected a type error.
